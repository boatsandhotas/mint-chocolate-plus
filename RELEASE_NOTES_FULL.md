# UAD:VP — Complete Feature List (Project History)

This is a complete, categorized list of every player-facing thing UAD:VP adds over vanilla Ultimate Admiral: Dreadnoughts, covering the mod's entire history from its first commit through the current build. It is a superset of `CHANGELOG.md` (which only covers changes since 0.5.104): everything built before that point — the foreign-nation design viewer, the in-game options menu and the dozens of individually toggleable balance changes it hosts, direct diplomacy actions, campaign status indicators, battle QoL and balance sliders, research and map options — is folded in here alongside the later eras (nation ship paints, invasions, ally ship purchase, naming themes, sub/patrol auto-assignment, surrendered-ship capture, ship service records, the shipbuilding-capacity overhaul) so this reads as one cumulative feature set, not a chronological log. Categories and prose depth match `CHANGELOG.md`'s own style.

Unless noted, features apply to the human player only and leave AI code paths on native behavior. UAD:VP is implemented entirely as runtime Harmony patches; it never edits the game's CSV/data files.

---

## Campaign

### Naval & land invasions
- **Launch naval invasions from an enemy-port popup.** A new *Launch Invasion* button on any enemy or minor-power port popup lets you order a seaborne invasion of that province yourself instead of waiting on the AI. Hidden for your own ports and where an invasion is already underway; shown greyed-out with a reason when you have under 25,000 tons of shipping in that sea area. Routed through vanilla's own conquest-event creation so save/load and AI awareness stay native; your task forces in the area supply the required tonnage. *(On)*
- **Launch land invasions from the province popup.** A *Launch Land Invasion* button lets you attack an adjacent enemy territory overland. Appears only when one of your provinces shares a confirmed mutual land border with the target (islands/non-bordering provinces are blocked), and re-enables after a failed assault. Creates a native `ProvinceBattle` (with its on-map arrow/flag) so the campaign, save/load, and AI treat it as vanilla-spawned. *(On)*
- **Reinforce a land invasion with your navy.** Click a land battle you're attacking or defending (on the map/globe hover popup, or its battle-popup force line) to toggle a naval-reinforcement commitment; `Ctrl+Shift+N`/`Ctrl+Shift+M` work as hotkey alternates. Your fleet tonnage parked at the nearest reachable coast (the target province's own port if it's coastal; otherwise the nearest port found by hopping land neighbors, with the bonus diminishing per inland hop — full at the coast, ~67% one hop in, ~50% two hops in) adds directly to the battle's real driving stat, `Player.ArmyForceForProvince`, so it affects both the advance/losses math and the native popup display in one shot. The bonus is uncapped — a large enough fleet can swing a land battle outright — but costs cash every turn the commitment stays active ($1,000 per committed ton/turn), charged automatically and stopping the moment you pull the fleet or cancel; commitments persist across saves and self-clear when the underlying battle ends. *(On by default at the "Strong" tier; "Naval Reinforcement" option is Off / Modest / Strong / Decisive, scaling both the per-ton force and the effective cap.)*
- **Enemy ports and province names now respond to clicks.** Vanilla ignores clicks on enemy/minor ports and territory labels; VP force-opens the full port window and province popup for them. This is what surfaces the invasion buttons, and as a side effect lets you open info popups for territory you don't own. *(On)*
- **Province popup pins in place when clicked.** The province popup is a cursor-following hover tooltip in vanilla, which made its invasion button impossible to click. Clicking a province name now latches the popup at a fixed position and adds an explicit Close button; plain hover still behaves as a normal tooltip, and stale pins self-heal if dismissed another way. *(On)*
- **Invasion diplomacy: war declaration, ally-betrayal penalty, and third-party blowback (with a preview).** Invading in peacetime auto-declares war (on the defender, or on the major protecting a minor) and applies tiered attitude penalties to every other major based on their stake in the target — allied with the defender, bordering the invaded province/country, holding territory in the same sea area, down to a flat general-aggression hit — each jittered +/-10% and scaled by current relations. Betraying a sworn ally cancels the alliance and adds a severe extra reputation hit. Justified counter-invasions (already at war, they invaded you, or one side occupies the other's claimed land) waive all third-party penalties. The confirmation dialog previews the war declaration, ally-betrayal warning, and each major's grouped penalty before you commit. *(On)*

### Surrendered-ship capture & vanquished spoils
- **Capture surrendered ships at battle end.** The victory-point winner now takes every surrendered, non-sunk ship — capturing the loser's surrendered vessels and recovering its own — instead of vanilla writing them all off as losses. The transfer is deferred until the campaign reconciles on World re-entry (with a loading-screen fallback) so it sticks, and kept ships are scrubbed from vanilla's post-battle loss pass. *(On; "Surrendered ship capture: On / Vanilla" toggle in options.)*
- **Vanquished-nation spoils on full conquest.** When an AI major is fully eliminated, its surviving fleet and a cash indemnity are distributed to the victors (majors holding its former territory, weighted by port capacity, plus a bonus to the "finisher" who took its last provinces), with only a fraction scuttled — instead of vanilla destroying the whole fleet and stranding the treasury. This removes the perverse incentive to leave a beaten nation a one-province rump so you can annex its assets. Majors only, never the human player. *(On; "Vanquished spoils share" Low/Medium/High option — Medium default: 40% of the fleet scuttled, 50% of cash seized.)*

### Shipbuilding capacity & port QoL
- **Shipyard capacity now follows conquest (multi-year rebuild).** Capturing a province from another major instantly strips the loser of that province's proportional share of its shipyard; the captor rebuilds that capacity gradually over a development-scaled number of years (roughly 0.5-6, faster for large/developed captures). Re-capturing a province mid-rebuild transfers only the portion developed so far — the undeveloped remainder is lost. A companion **Overseas Capacity Weight** option (Low/Medium/High, Medium default) sets how much colonial territory counts toward capacity versus the homeland. *(On; vanilla left capacity unchanged on territory transfer.)*
- **Higher total shipbuilding capacity for all players.** Multiplies the total shipbuilding-capacity limit for every player (human and AI) so a single large design no longer eats a huge fraction of a restrictively low vanilla cap. Applies uniformly across UI, build gating, overcapacity penalties, and AI scrap targets. *(On; Vanilla / 1.5x / 2x / 3x in options, 2x default.)*
- **Foreign ports contribute to shipbuilding capacity too.** Vanilla's shipbuilding-capacity limit only counts a nation's home-province ports. VP adds every port-capacity point held in non-home (captured or ceded) provinces on top of vanilla's result, scaled by the game's own shipbuilding-capacity-ratio parameter and a VP multiplier — 0.5x in "Half" mode (default) or 0x in "Vanilla" mode, which fully restores the original home-ports-only calculation. Applies equally to human and AI nations. *(On; "Foreign Port Capacity" 50%/Vanilla toggle, 50% default.)*
- **Suspend Dock Overcapacity instead of a flat slowdown.** Vanilla applies one global time penalty to every job at an over-capacity shipyard, uniformly slowing repairs, refits, and new builds alike. VP instead works out how much of the month's tonnage actually fits current capacity, runs that work at full speed — near-finished repairs first, then builds/refits by remaining months — and simply pauses whatever doesn't fit rather than penalizing it, so one huge overflow build no longer cripples every other job in the yard. *(On; "Suspend Dock Overcapacity" Automatic/Manual toggle, Automatic default.)*
- **Send a whole multi-selected group to one port in one click.** Raises vanilla's small per-click limit in the Fleet tab's Change Port flow so an entire multi-selected group can be ordered to a single port at once. *(Always on.)*
- **Ship counts on campaign-map port labels.** Port name labels on the map now show a bold "(N)" ship-count suffix (and switch from muted to bold/dark text) whenever vessels are stationed or passing through, matching the port popup's own inventory instead of leaving labels generic. *(Always on.)*

### Task forces & logistics (new)
- **AI task-force staging merges scattered groups before they collide piecemeal.** After each AI nation's own task-force movement resolves, VP scans that nation's moving Ship/Submarine task forces (excluding anything already tied to a battle, without supply, or on an invalid path) and buckets them by vessel type and destination "theater" (the destination port's province area, or a rounded map-coordinate bucket if no port is targeted). Within a cluster of two or more eligible groups it picks the largest (ties go to the slowest) as an anchor, halts it in place, and redirects the other groups onto its exact position so vanilla's own arrival-merge logic folds them into one task force before any battle spawns — instead of each group fighting piecemeal as it straggles in. A cluster only proceeds if it clears a minimum floor (submarines: 3+ vessels; surface: 3+ vessels or 5,000+ combined tons), and a redirect is only taken if the merged group stays under the nation's normal task-force size caps; a separate pass also converges moving stragglers onto an already-stationary friendly group of the same type within 1 map unit of their destination. Explicitly skips the human player's own nation. *(On; "AI Task Force Staging" Staging/Vanilla toggle, Staging default.)*
- **Army Logistics: a real formula behind the nation logistics stat.** In "Balanced" mode (default), replaces the game's own per-nation logistics-factor calculation for every nation, human and AI alike, with `100 × transportCoverage × navyCoverage` (capped at 100 for majors, 30 for medium powers, 15 for minors), where `navyCoverage` is half power-rating coverage and half tonnage coverage against requirements that scale with a nation's home ports, foreign ports, and non-home provinces (e.g. required fleet tonnage = 100,000 + 3,000/home port + 6,000/foreign port + 4,000/non-home province). "Vanilla" restores the original budget/population-based formula, including its random roll for non-major nations. This is a background stat the game's own army/invasion logic consumes elsewhere; no new UI beyond the toggle. *(On; "Army Logistics" Balanced/Vanilla toggle, Balanced default.)*
- **Campaign Naval Mobility: faster task-force travel for everyone.** Rewrites the shared `monthly_ship_speed_mod` parameter that drives both campaign-map task-force travel distance per month and how far a task force can range from its supply port, applied identically to every nation. Four presets: Extended = 0.8 (default; per the option's own tooltip, roughly 2.7x vanilla monthly movement), Fast = 0.65, Improved = 0.5, Vanilla = 0.2975 (a hardcoded stand-in for the original value — selecting "Vanilla" still actively re-applies the parameter rather than skipping the patch). *(On; "Campaign Naval Mobility" Extended/Fast/Improved/Vanilla toggle, Extended default.)*
- **Task Force Sustainment: campaign-layer fuel/ammo/supply attrition effectively eliminated.** In "Full" mode (default), every nation's task forces are always treated as supplied regardless of distance from a supply port, monthly fuel consumption is intercepted and replaced with a full top-off, the end-of-turn replenishment pass tops off every ship's fuel/ammo through the game's own methods called with an effectively unlimited capacity argument (bypassing normal port-capacity throttling), and every ship is refueled/rearmed to full at both battle acceptance and battle completion; a direct-fill fallback (fuel to 100, AP/HE to max) catches anything the normal replenish call still leaves short. Applies to every nation, not just the human player. "Vanilla" restores the original attrition. *(On; "Task Force Sustainment" Full/Vanilla toggle, Full default.)*
- **Sea Transport Losses scoped to active vessels only.** Vanilla's periodic abstract "transport losses" roll (vessels written off in a sea area from enemy presence) counts every vessel the game considers "in the area" for a nation, including fleets merely transiting under movement orders. VP snapshots every vessel currently in a moving task force at the start of each turn's loss-processing window and filters them out of the area/nation vessel count for that window, so only vessels genuinely stationed or patrolling in a sea area can be rolled as a loss. Applies equally to human and AI nations. *(On; "Sea Transport Losses" Active Forces/Vanilla toggle, Active Forces default.)*

### Submarine & patrol auto-assignment
- **Minelayer Port Goals panel (F8).** Set a default per-port minelayer composition (count of each sub variant) plus per-port overrides or a "skip this port" marker. *Rebalance now* places only idle/unassigned minelayer subs into quota-deficit ports, leaving assigned and in-transit subs untouched. A per-variant *Build N + assign* action builds the missing subs (with a shipyard-capacity-over-budget warning) and homes them. Goals persist per campaign. *(On)*
- **Patrol / Foreign Stations panel (F7).** See your controlled regions ranked by tonnage deficit (required defense minus current), multi-select your in-port or building ships (by ship, shift-click, or whole class), then *Send* them to a chosen region/port or *Auto-distribute* the selection so each ship goes to the currently-neediest region and the load spreads out. Contract hulls being built for other nations are excluded. *(On)*

### Ship service records
- **Persistent per-ship career battle log.** Every player ship keeps a career service record across the whole campaign. Each battle records absolute damage dealt and received, ships sunk (finishing blow) and wrecked (most damage to a ship that went down), whether it survived, and a per-enemy breakdown (each foe's class, tonnage, and damage taken). Records are keyed by ship id so they keep accumulating across refits and saves. *(On)*
- **Ship Service Records viewer (F10 panel).** A draggable, always-on-top campaign-only panel: a sortable, scrollable ship list on the left (sort by tonnage sunk, kills, battles fought, name, or class); the selected ship's career totals on the right, including tonnage sunk/damaged broken down by enemy type, plus a newest-first battle history where each row expands to reveal the specific enemy ships sunk, wrecked, or hit that battle. *(Gated behind the Ship Service Records option, On by default.)*

### Diplomacy
- **Declare War button on politics rows.** Adds a direct *Declare War* button (with a confirmation dialog) to each nation's row in the campaign politics screen, so starting a war no longer requires manufacturing a casus belli through the vanilla tension system. Uses vanilla's own attitude-transition path (a large forced attitude swing) so war declaration, dates, events, and allied war side-effects all run through code the game already trusts. Blocked against yourself or a nation you're already at war with. *(Always available.)*
- **Force Peace button on politics rows.** A companion *Force Peace* button ends an existing war on the spot (blocked if vanilla's own `CanSignPeace` says a treaty is currently disallowed by campaign events). If one side holds a clear victory-point lead, VP first runs vanilla's own reparation transfer between the winner and loser before flipping the relation to peace via the same attitude-transition path vanilla's peace treaties use — so a forced peace still pays out like a real one instead of just switching off the war flag. *(Always available.)*

### World, research & map options
- **Mine Warfare on/off.** Disabling suppresses all minefield damage and hides mine-related parts/components in the constructor, without touching minefields or mounted mine gear already present in an existing save. *(Vanilla/Enabled by default; toggle in options.)*
- **Submarine Warfare on/off.** Disabling blocks new submarine construction (player and AI) and new submarine-battle generation, and dismisses in-progress submarine battles/damage — without deleting submarines your campaign already has. *(Vanilla/Enabled by default; toggle in options.)*
- **Technology Spread modes: catch-up diffusion or a fully historical research model.** Gradual/Swift/Unrestricted give trailing majors a per-tech-category research-speed multiplier that scales with how far behind the current leader they are (Unrestricted's table is roughly four times as generous as Gradual's), narrowing the tech gap without touching the leader's own speed. Historical instead replaces spending-based research entirely with a deterministic, calendar-driven model — research speed and AI tech budgets are zeroed out so campaign year alone governs ordinary technology availability, and each turn's sync now cross-checks every nation against the game's full technology catalog rather than just techs already in that nation's own list, so a tech whose calendar year has passed gets created and marked researched even if vanilla's own unlock chain never surfaced it for that nation. *(Vanilla by default; five-way option.)*
- **Research standing badge.** Each technology category row (and the window itself) in the campaign Research window gets an appended "(Ahead by N techs)" / "(Behind by N techs)" / "(Tied)" badge comparing your progress in that category to the best major, answering "am I ahead or behind?" without leaving the window. *(Always on.)*
- **Continue past the vanilla 1965 retirement.** Suppresses vanilla's hard "Campaign Finished: Retirement" popup and blocks the underlying `FinishCampaign(Retirement)` call once the campaign reaches 1965, so play continues indefinitely; defeat and bankruptcy end-conditions are untouched. *(On by default; toggle in options.)*
- **Early canal openings (Panama & Kiel).** Optionally moves both canals' availability year to 1890 (instead of their historical 1914 and 1895) so early-game fleets can use them, then lets vanilla's own canal-obstacle and route-passability logic take over from there. *(Historical by default; "Early" toggle in options.)*
- **Campaign map wraparound (Disc World).** An early wraparound mode for the flat campaign map: duplicates the rendered map surface and passive visual layers at the visible seam and proxies map interactions across it, plus a seamless-rotation pass, so the world reads as continuous at the edges instead of hard-stopping. Superseded/extended by the later three-way Map Geometry selector (Flat Earth / Disc World / Globe) described under Experimental, where Disc World is one of the three modes. *(Off by default.)*

### Campaign status indicators & fleet QoL
- **In-game UAD:VP options menu.** A button added to the top-right vanilla options row opens a sectioned settings panel (Battle / Campaign / Ship Design, later expanded further) hosting every individually toggleable balance option in this list — no config files, no loose ini; every switch lives in-game and persists via the OS-level settings store. *(Always available.)*
- **Campaign construction summary: own vs. foreign contracts vs. commissioning.** The campaign country-info panel's build count now shows three exclusive queue buckets — hulls you're building for yourself, hulls your yards are building under contract for another nation (greyed), and ships commissioning — instead of vanilla's single opaque total, with a tooltip explaining the split. *(Always on.)*
- **Active fleet in-port count.** The campaign Active Fleet summary is annotated with how many of those vessels are currently sitting in port (e.g. "42 (11 port)"), answering "how much of my fleet is actually deployed?" without opening fleet details. *(Always on.)*
- **Dock expansion & transport capacity status.** The country-info panel gains a compact line showing whether a dock expansion is in progress (with elapsed/total months and the planned capacity increase) and your current transport capacity as a color-coded percentage (green ≥190%, amber ≥150%, red below), with a tooltip explaining both and calling out the ~200% transport target. *(Always on.)*
- **Technology "next discovery" indicator.** The country-info technology line gets a "(Next Nm)" estimate for the soonest tech about to complete, computed the same way vanilla's own research window derives its per-tech month countdowns, so you don't have to open Research to check. *(Always on.)*
- **Task-force tonnage indicator on the map.** Campaign-map task-force icons get a bottom-up, friendly/enemy-tinted fill layered under the normal icon silhouette that fills in proportion to the group's battle tonnage (maxed out around 100,000 tons), so relative fleet size is visible at a glance without opening the popup. *(Always on.)*
- **One-click "Return to Port" on task forces.** The task-force popup gains a *Return to `<origin port>`* button that routes the group back to its saved origin in a single click — only enabled when it's your own task force, you still control that port, and it isn't already there or already ordered back. *(Always available.)*
- **Auto-resolve win-chance odds in the battle popup.** Appends "Auto-resolve win chance: N%" to the campaign battle popup's mission description, computed read-only from vanilla's own `CampaignBattle.VictoryChance` (never calling the mutating auto-resolve routine itself), and is stripped again once the result popup shows. *(Always on.)*

### Other campaign tools
- **Switch Nation (defect to another major mid-campaign).** A new *Switch Nation* tab in the in-game options lets you hand your current nation to the AI and take over any other major, continuing the same campaign as them. Pick a target with `<`/`>`, arm and confirm; the mod flips `isMain`/`isAi` on both nations, carries your campaign-id GUID onto the new nation, saves, and drops to the main menu to Continue as the new nation. Blocked during battle and when no other majors exist.
- **Bundled ship-name theme database.** An embedded name-theme database (`ship_names.csv` baked into the DLL, no loose config) supplies the selectable naming themes: per-country national fleets for roughly ten nations, universal themes usable by everyone (e.g. Greek Gods, optionally culture-gated to owners), and conquered-territory themes that let you name a class from another nation's pool once you hold its territory. National themes shadow same-named universal ones, and available themes are filtered to the class.

## AI

- **AI majors fund transport, then tech, then crew training.** AI majors now redistribute their already-chosen naval budget by a priority ladder — merchant/transport capacity up to 200%, then technology, then crew training — instead of vanilla starving transport (observed around 8% when ~200% is healthy). It only reallocates the existing total (never raises spend, bankruptcy-safe) and applies to AI majors only, never the human. *(On)*
- **Advanced AI Builder: refresh stale AI designs instead of getting stuck on them.** When an AI major's current design for a ship type has gone stale (older than about a year), VP forces a fresh design-generation pass for that type and bypasses vanilla's replacement-count gate that would otherwise block the refresh — so AI navies keep modernizing their designs instead of endlessly re-building the same obsolete class. AI majors only. *(On by default.)*
- **AI Fleet Mix: shape what type mix AI majors build.** Rewrites the loaded per-type build-ratio weights AI majors draw from (Balanced = a flat ratio across BB/BC/CA/CL/DD/TB; Heavy = weighted toward capital ships and cruisers over destroyers/torpedo boats) and layers a deficit-weighted picker on top of vanilla's next-ship-type roll, so an AI major's fleet actively grows toward its target composition instead of vanilla's flatter, more random type selection. The picker now also probes each candidate design against vanilla's own buildability check before counting it, so a type whose only designs are stale or no longer buildable is skipped instead of being falsely treated as a viable pick. AI majors only. *(Heavy by default; Vanilla/Balanced/Heavy option.)*

### AI design & refit automation (new)
- **Smart AI Designs (experimental AI new-design generator).** When an AI nation's shared-design and predefined-design attempts both fail for a ship type, vanilla falls back to a random generator that can produce overweight or invalid hulls. With this on, VP intercepts that fallback and runs up to 3 deterministic attempts instead: each takes the nation's own random hull template, runs it through vanilla's actual part-fitting routine (stopped early once its "smart parts" step succeeds), backfills gun/tower-appropriate component defaults and Smart-Refit-style armor, and — for destroyers and torpedo boats only — runs a weight-rescue ladder (strip barbette, strip anti-flooding, standard bulkheads, single-bottom hull, then drop survivability to Medium then Very Low) if the hull is over its weight/cost/barbette limit. The finished hull must pass vanilla buildability with no grandfathering waivers, and must be competitive: its cost/speed/armor/weapon-weighted power score must place in the nation's existing top 3 designs of that type, or reach at least 90% of the #3 design's score, else it's discarded. If all 3 attempts fail, the AI simply doesn't get a new design that cycle. AI-controlled nations only. *(Off by default; "Smart AI Designs" Experimental/Vanilla toggle.)*
- **AI design-roster cap keeps AI design books to the 3 strongest per type.** Before each AI nation's new-construction step, VP prunes its design book down to the 3 strongest designs per surface type (BB/BC/CA/CL/DD/TB), ranked buildable-first then by the same adjusted-power score Smart AI Designs uses, deleting the rest (skipping any design still protected as a live refit target). Runs unconditionally for AI nations, independent of the Smart AI Designs toggle, though Smart AI Designs' own competitiveness gate reuses this ranking. *(Always on; no separate setting.)*
- **Smart Refits replace the AI's refit coroutine outright.** With "Smart Refits" enabled (default), vanilla's own AI-refit logic is fully bypassed for every AI major that isn't the player's own nation. Each turn per AI nation: ships already mid-refit get more sister ships folded in automatically; otherwise, subject to a 25% base chance roll and cash of at least half the nation's monthly revenue, up to 2 new refit groups are started (capped at 8 ships each), drawn only from surface combatants at least 4 years old with a working main battery. The oldest ship in a class is cloned into a new design and run through the same engine as the player's own manual Smart Refit button but with every stage enabled: guns are rebuilt and revalidated, all 14 hull-internal component slots (engine, boilers, rudder, barbette, torpedo belt, etc.) are upgraded to the best currently-unlocked part, and armor is regenerated by the constructor's Generate Armor engine running in a cheaper search mode. The result must pass full buildability/cost validation and score strictly higher adjusted power than the ship it replaces, or the attempt rolls back to the ship's exact pre-refit state via a snapshot — an AI refit is either a validated upgrade or it never happens. *(On by default; "Smart Refits" Enhanced/Vanilla toggle.)*

## Battle

- **Division speed-sync: followers hold the leader's manual speed.** When you set a manual speed on a player division's leader, every follower is forced to that same speed (capped to its own max) instead of racing ahead and looping/doing 360s to fall back into line; releasing the leader's manual speed returns followers to normal formation speed. Player divisions only, AI-controlled ships skipped. *(On)*
- **Reverse-course hotkeys (R = port, T = starboard).** Press R to turn every selected division 180 degrees to port, T to starboard. The maneuver is chosen in options: Single 180, "90 / swap / 90" (default), "Split & rejoin" (temporarily splits each follower into its own division so all ships start the turn on the same frame, then rejoins them reversed), or Rudder (experimental hard-over per ship, falls back to 90/swap/90 if it can't start). Column order is reversed and the group UI refreshed so the new lead ship shows in front. Also backs the HELM panel's on-screen Port/Stbd buttons. *(Player-initiated, always available.)*
- **Parallel station-keeping order (Shift+P / new PAR button).** Select a division, press Shift+P or click the new PAR button in the order bar, then click an anchor division — the selected division continuously runs parallel to that anchor, offset either astern and toward the disengaged (away-from-enemy) side (good for a DD torpedo lurk or a trailing line) or on its beam (abreast, via an options toggle; astern default). Any genuine new order, manual move, follow/scout/screen order, or retreat cancels it.
- **Hand a division back to AI control, mid-battle (hotkey 6).** Adds an "AI" button to the battle order row (also bound to hotkey 6) that toggles selected friendly divisions between manual and AI control for the rest of the fight, without touching campaign ownership or the enemy's AI. A manual right-click order (or reissuing any real order) automatically clears the toggle for the affected division. *(Always available.)*
- **Battle speed stays at your chosen setting.** Vanilla automatically slows simulation speed as a proximity/detection safeguard; VP raises the effective ceiling (up to 30x) and re-applies your last chosen speed on top of vanilla's automatic slowdown attempts, so the game doesn't quietly throttle you back mid-fight. *(Always on.)*
- **Space bar gives a clean pause/resume in battle.** Replaces vanilla's Space handling with a predictable toggle: pausing remembers your current non-zero time scale and resuming restores exactly that speed, instead of vanilla's less predictable interaction with the speed slider. *(Always on.)*
- **Battle Weather: force clear daytime skies.** An "Always Sunny" option clamps the battle's time-of-day, weather, wind, and storm intensity to calm daytime conditions right after vanilla's own weather roll runs, so routine fights aren't randomly fought at night or in a storm. Vanilla's own weather setup logic still runs first; VP only overrides the final state. *(On by default; Always Sunny / Vanilla toggle.)*
- **Battle Spotting Range multiplier.** Multiplies the spotting/detection range every ship uses to find targets (3x/5x/10x, or Vanilla), buffing only the spotting side of detection and leaving target visibility, weather, and smoke concealment on vanilla rules. *(3x by default.)*
- **Battle Damage multiplier.** Scales the game's global gun and torpedo section-damage parameters by a flat 2x/3x/5x (or Vanilla), applied once at game-data load (and re-applied on option change outside of battle) rather than hooked into any per-shell combat path, so fights resolve faster and more decisively. *(3x by default.)*
- **Realistic Shell Damage curve.** Replaces vanilla's flatter, sometimes spiky per-caliber damage curve with one that scales roughly with shell volume (caliber cubed, anchored at 12-inch), rewriting the loaded gun-damage data once rather than hooking combat math, so bigger guns consistently hit harder relative to small ones instead of vanilla's small-gun/super-heavy-gun anomalies. *(Realistic by default; Realistic / Vanilla toggle.)*
- **Battle weapon rows show live target range.** Each weapon row in the battle ship-info panel that's showing a hit-chance percentage gets a second line underneath giving that weapon's current target range in kilometers (read from the weapon's own aim data, or a straight-line fallback), refreshed continuously as the row updates; rows without a resolvable target are left unchanged. *(Always on.)*

## Constructor / UI

### Class naming themes (new)
- **Assign a name theme per ship class in the constructor.** A new *Theme* button lets you assign a naming theme to a ship's class. New ships of the class then draw names from the chosen theme's pool (deconflicted against names already in use) instead of the generic per-nation list, or from a sequential "`<Class>`-N" numbering scheme. Choices save per campaign, keyed by the class's real refit base-name so all refits share the theme, and apply to the human player only. *(Controlled by a "Class Naming Themes" On/Off option that defaults On but has no effect until you assign a theme; Off also hides the button and reverts to vanilla naming.)*
- **Assigning a theme retroactively renames the whole class family.** Choosing a theme immediately renames the entire class family: all matching design templates and the lead ship take the theme's first name, and remaining ships take successive theme names, each keeping its own refit-date suffix (e.g. "(Jul. 1904)") so refit variants stay dated. Human player only; the class assignment is re-keyed to the new lead name so future builds stay themed.

### Constructor automation tools (new)
- **Generate Armor button.** Adds a "Generate Armor" button to the constructor that computes a coherent armor profile from the current ship in one click, spending remaining tonnage into armor without ever reacting automatically to hull/gun/component/tonnage/speed changes — the player must click it again after such a change. It resets every armor zone to its legal minimum, then binary-searches a single scale factor (32 iterations) that proportionally raises each zone's target ratio (belt ~1.0, bow/stern belt ~0.38-0.42, deck ~0.125-0.18) as far as the tonnage budget (current cap minus a 1-ton margin) allows; main-gun turret faces (10-inch+ calibers) are floored at caliber-thickness with no cap, while secondary guns get flat, capped armor so they can't cannibalize the main belt. Leftover tonnage is spent in follow-up fine/overflow fill passes (time-capped at 2 seconds). No in-game warning if the budget can't be fully spent. Human player's own editable design only. *(Always on.)*
- **Auto Design Lite button.** Adds an "Auto Lite" button beside vanilla's Random Ship button that re-places parts on the current hull without touching tonnage, speed, beam, draught, range, armor, or components — unlike vanilla Auto Design/Random Ship, which re-rolls the hull and those sliders first. It strips the ship's current parts and re-runs vanilla's own part-placement routine directly against the hull as-is; if the result comes out overweight, VP trims conservatively (zero out custom gun-caliber bonuses, then drop the smallest-caliber secondary-gun group, up to 4 groups) before giving up. Human player's own editable design only. *(Always on.)*
- **Smart Refit button (player, component defaults only).** A "Smart Refit" button on the human player's own editable refit design updates every upgradeable component slot (engine, fuel, boilers, rudder, armor/barbette/torpedo-belt hardware, etc.) to the best currently-unlocked part for that hull, without rebuilding guns or armor — a lighter, manual version of the same engine AI majors run automatically (see Smart Refits, under AI). *(Always on.)*
- **Practical new-hull defaults.** Picking or swapping a hull in the constructor no longer hands you vanilla's randomized post-change state: VP sets speed to the hull's own top speed (clamped to the ship type's legal range), tonnage to the maximum the hull/type/player limit allows, range to Very High, crew quarters to Spacious, crew training to 100%, and every upgradeable component slot to the best currently-unlocked part; once a gun, main tower, or torpedo launcher is actually placed, matching armament/equipment/torpedo defaults follow. Scoped to the human player's own hull-change path only — Auto Design, AI generation, imports, and save loads keep their own initialization behavior. *(Always on.)*

### Fleet UI fixes / QoL
- **Reworked ship "Pwr" power rating (throw-weight model).** Replaces vanilla's misranking `EstimatePower` as the displayed base power with a throw-weight model: per-salvo shell damage (scaling roughly with caliber cubed) times a hull-size/armor survivability term, so capital ships outrank light fast-firing cruisers and "fewer, bigger guns" raises power. Only Belt and Deck armor are read (other zones carry garbage sentinels on light hulls). Native influence/PowerProjection is left untouched. *(On, no in-game toggle.)*

### Design tools & balance
- **Foreign design viewer.** A nation-flag toolbar in the campaign Fleet window's Designs tab lets you click any AI major's flag to browse that nation's own ship designs (read-only — build/refit/delete stay disabled for foreign owners), with a per-flag tooltip summarizing that nation's design and ship counts. Ship-count columns show active/building(foreign contracts)/other tallies instead of vanilla's single count, and erased/obsolete designs still referenced by in-service ships remain visible instead of disappearing from your own list. *(Always on.)*
- **CA+ torpedo restriction.** Optionally removes torpedo launchers from the part-availability list for heavy cruisers, battlecruisers, and battleships — for AI design generation as well as the human constructor, not just the player's own part list — so major combatants stop feeling like oversized torpedo boats. A retroactive cleanup pass still strips already-fitted torpedo launchers (and their support components) from existing AI-owned CA+/BC/BB ships and designs as a defensive backstop for old saves, imports, and any generation path that slips through, and now also forces a weapon-cache refresh so a ship's internal "has torpedoes" flag can't stay stuck true after its launchers are removed. Human-designed ships you already built are left alone. *(Disallowed by default; toggle in options.)*
- **Hull Speed Adjustment reins in overpowered early torpedo-boat and destroyer hulls.** Caps the hull speed limiter at 26 kn on the three early TB hulls (`tb_lowbow`, `tb_highbow`, `tb_standard`) and at 29 kn on eight early DD hulls (the `dd_1` family and its national variants), replacing whichever higher value vanilla shipped, and re-gates the early oversized funnel part (`torpedo_funnel_3`) behind its proper tech unlock instead of being available from the start. A companion runtime check further hides 3-tube dual-funnel torpedo mounts from the constructor's part-availability list before 1894 (by the design's own year), so the live build screen enforces the same limit the loaded data does, not just new games. Applies to the shared hull/part data every designer — human and AI — draws from. *(On; "Hull Speed Adjustment" Adjusted/Vanilla toggle, Adjusted default.)*
- **Hull Weight Adjustment: one toggle, three weight tables.** Caps each hull's mass-ratio share of total ship weight to a per-class ceiling (BB 0.16, BC 0.18, CA 0.20, CL 0.22, DD 0.24, TB 0.26 — only hulls above the cap are pulled down); strips inflated weight penalties baked into protection technologies (deletes several late-game anti-torpedo/citadel/armor-forging weight surcharges outright, and softens two multi-bottom-hull weight penalties); and flattens crew weight to a constant 0.2 while rebalancing six torpedo-boat gun-tower weights and re-gating the four larger towers behind their proper tech unlock. All three pieces ride the same single "Hull Weight Adjustment" toggle — there's no separate setting for the protection-tech or crew/tower pieces, and the in-game tooltip only describes the hull-ratio-cap piece. A companion runtime check also hides oversized normal-TB torpedo towers from the constructor before 1900 (by the design's own year). Applies to the shared part/tech data every designer draws from. *(On; "Hull Weight Adjustment" Adjusted/Vanilla toggle, Adjusted default.)*
- **Refit names read as dates.** Refit designs get a readable "(Jul. 1904)" month-and-year suffix instead of vanilla's flat "-2" duplicate-name counter or a bare year that collided often enough to need letter suffixes ("1904", "1904a", "1904b"); a trailing letter still appears for the rare same-month collision so no name is ever silently overwritten. *(Always on.)*
- **Shared Designs quick-access — now a 4-way policy with a new Only mode.** Exposes vanilla's own campaign Shared-Designs setting directly from the UAD:VP options menu instead of requiring a separate settings screen; for Off/Selective/Always this remains a straight convenience relocation of vanilla's existing three-way setting, not a new mechanic. VP adds a fourth mode, Only, implemented as vanilla's Always plus a hidden VP-only flag layered on top (the campaign save's own setting field never actually gains a 4th value): under Always, an AI nation that finds no acceptable shared design for a ship type still falls back to a vanilla-random (or VP's own Smart AI Designs) design as usual; under Only, that fallback is blocked outright, so the AI simply produces no new design for that ship type on that attempt rather than ever building anything but an accepted shared design. Changes future AI design-generation attempts only — a nation's existing roster designs are untouched, and the human player is unaffected. *(Vanilla by default; 4-way option: Off / Selective / Always / Only — Only is VP-original.)*
- **Shared Designs constructor: exact year entry, nation flags, and Historical-Research-consistent tech.** In the Shared Designs build/browse screen, vanilla's coarse year stepper and country-arrow selector are replaced with a typeable exact-year field (clamped 1890-1960) and a click-to-pick grid of nation flags, and the screen now defaults to your current campaign year (or your last pick) instead of vanilla's own starting point. Separately, when Technology Spread is set to Historical, this same screen computes tech availability the same deterministic, calendar-driven way the rest of the campaign does under that mode, instead of vanilla's per-session randomized tech-unlock offset — so a design saved for a given year can't quietly include a tech that only unlocked early because of vanilla's randomness. The year/flag picker is UI-only; vanilla still owns shared-design refresh and filtering. *(Always on for the picker; the Historical-Research tie-in only changes anything when Technology Spread = Historical.)*

## Experimental (opt-in, off by default unless noted)

### Nation ship paints
- **Experimental Nation Ship Paints mode.** An opt-in mode (default **Off**) that recolors ships with nation-themed paint schemes in the constructor preview and in battle. Ten built-in national base looks (USA, UK, Germany, France, Russia, Japan, Italy, Austria-Hungary, Spain, China) tint hull, superstructure, and guns while preserving texture detail; turning it Off restores the game's original ship materials.
- **Ship Paints settings pane with per-nation swatches.** While paint mode is on, a *Ship Paints* tab lists every supported nation as a row of clickable per-channel color swatches with a channel-label header and a per-nation Reset. Hidden when the toggle is Off.
- **Eight per-channel paint targets.** Hull, Superstructure, Turret (guns/barbettes), Deck, Bottom (below waterline), Detail (roof/metal fittings), Barrel, and Trim — classified per-material so one part can carry several separately tinted surfaces.
- **Constructor paint panel: separate Nation vs per-Class colors.** A paint launcher button in the constructor opens a panel with two swatch rows — nation-wide colors and per-class (this design) colors layered on top — so a class can be painted differently from the rest of the fleet. Per-class overrides are stored per design GUID; only the channels a class customizes replace the nation values.
- **Promote / Demote / Swap between class and nation paint.** The class row adds Promote (copy this class's overrides up into the nation so future classes inherit them), Demote (clear class overrides, falling back to the nation scheme), and Swap (exchange the nation and class paint strings).
- **Color picker.** Clicking any swatch opens a live-preview picker: a draggable hue/saturation wheel, brightness/value slider, typed `#RRGGBB` hex input, black/white quick swatches, a row of naval preset colors, and up to six saveable custom presets (Save to capture, shift-click a custom swatch to delete). Edits preview on the ship immediately.

### Buy ships from an allied major (new)
- **Buy Ship from an ally.** With the feature enabled, viewing an allied major's ship designs in the campaign fleet design viewer adds a *Buy Ship* button that opens a quote dialog: pick how many hulls (capped by your cash for the deposit and the ally's willingness) and see per-hull price, build time, and the deposit/balance split. The ally builds the hulls in their own dock at a premium over the design's build cost (band configurable Low/Med/High, default Medium ~+50%..+120%, positioned by the seller's dock pressure); you pay a 30% deposit up front and the balance as each hull commissions, at which point ownership transfers to you. Orders and purchased-class records persist across save/reload. *(Off by default.)*
- **Incoming ally-built ships in the Fleet tab.** Ships an ally is building for you appear as rows pinned to the top of the Fleet tab, styled like vanilla's "building for another nation" rows (yellow "Building x%, Nm" / "Fitting out" status, the ally in the Sold column, total price in Cost). Each row has a Select Port control for delivery (you're also prompted right after ordering to set the port for the batch), and clicking a row lets you cancel the order (deposit forfeit, ally keeps the hull). These seller-owned hulls don't count against your own shipbuilding capacity. *(Off)*
- **Purchased ship classes are refit-only.** A class bought from an ally may be refitted but never queued as a new build in your own yards; the build gate is forced to fail for the purchased design and its entire refit lineage ("Purchased from ally — refit only; buy more from your ally to build new."). To get more hulls you must reorder from the ally. *(Off)*
- **Alliance-break disposition for outstanding orders.** If your alliance with a seller ends mid-build: breaking into war lets the ally seize the in-progress hull and forfeits your deposit; a peaceful split honors the contract so the ship still delivers on completion. Already-delivered ships are unaffected. *(Off)*

### Battle experimentals
- **Auto-apply preferred per-ship-type settings at battle start.** Applies your saved per-type preferences each fight: ammo (AP/HE/Auto) and fire-torpedoes per ship, plus avoid-torpedoes, avoid-ships, auto group-leader, and Line/Column formation keyed by the division leader's type. Heavies (BB/BC/CA) default to AP, others to HE; "Leave" keeps vanilla. Re-applies to new divisions from a mid-battle split without overwriting manual changes. *(Off — opt-in preference.)*
- **Follow-steering damping to reduce the follower "weave."** Optional per-follower yaw damping that soft-clips only the excess turn rate above a threshold to kill the S-pattern weave a fast hull with a slow rudder shows while station-keeping. Leaders are untouched; ships far off-station (rejoining) and ships actively dodging torpedoes are exempted so they keep full rudder, and it never fights a commanded reverse maneuver. *(Off)*

### Design experimentals
- **Superstructure Compatibility: relax tower/funnel availability across a hull family.** When a tower or funnel part isn't available on your current hull because of an unmet design requirement, VP checks whether a newer, already-unlocked hull of the same broad class (capital/cruiser/screen/submarine) would satisfy that requirement, and if so allows the part anyway — effectively letting historically-paired superstructures appear a little early on related hulls within the same class family once the tech exists somewhere in it. Constructor-only, human player only; vanilla's own gate is only ever relaxed, never tightened. *(Off by default.)*

### Other experimentals
- **Globe campaign-map mode (3D sphere skin).** The Map Geometry option becomes a three-way selector (Flat Earth / Disc World / Globe). Globe mode renders the campaign map as a true 3D political sphere built from the game's own country meshes re-projected by lat/long, orbited with right-drag/scroll, while the underlying simulation stays flat. No external assets. Border lines and great-circle movement are not represented. *(Off — Flat default.)*
- **Ship Resupply Override (top up stranded fleets).** Force-refuels and rearms all your ships on demand via the game's own resupply routines with unlimited free capacity, for task forces stranded at sea. Adds a "Ship Resupply Override" On/Off toggle plus a "Resupply My Fleet Now" button in options. *(Off)*

## Fixes

- **Per-turn weight/cost crash from null gun caliber data.** Stability guard preventing a null `turretPartData` in a ship's gun-caliber list from throwing a `NullReferenceException` in `GunData.BaseWeight` and aborting the campaign's per-turn weight/cost update for the entire fleet. At the `Ship.CWeight` chokepoint it asks vanilla to reconcile calibers, drops any still-bad entries, and logs the offending ship. *(Always on, no toggle.)*
- **Shared Designs browser freeze on broken designs.** Stops the main-menu Shared Designs browser from freezing when a community design references parts the current game data can no longer grade (e.g. a missing gun grade). Finalizers turn the hard freeze into a graceful skip so the broken design just fails to render; the constructor-exception swallow is scoped to the shared-design browser only. *(Always on.)*
- **Keep my fleet refittable: player refits no longer vanish from the Design tab.** When one of your ships' designs went obsolete, vanilla's cull erased the template and any refit cloned from it was born erased, so the new refit never appeared in the Ship Design tab. VP now skips the obsolescence cull for the main human player and un-erases the in-flight refit clone so vanilla commits the refit normally. A follow-up guard now also catches this at the game's general ship-clone routine (`PlayerController.CloneShipRaw`) itself, un-erasing a clone there too whenever both it and its source would otherwise inherit erased status — closing the same gap for any clone-to-design path beyond the specific refit-menu flow. Human player only; AI design pipelines untouched. *(Always on, narrowly scoped to avoid the save corruption the old bulk un-eraser caused.)*
- **No more duplicate ship rows in the port popup.** Fixes the port popup listing some ships twice (a vessel that is both stationed and part of a task force in the port) by hiding exact-duplicate rows and keeping the first. The port tonnage total was already correct. *(Always on.)*
- **British late-hull tower availability corrected.** A handful of British G3/N3/Hood-class late-hull towers were missing the need-tag/unlock-tech references that pair them with their historical hulls, so they never showed up in the constructor even once the normal tech gates were met. VP patches the loaded part data to add the missing references. *(Always on, no toggle.)*
- **Campaign ship-list formatting no longer crashes on a malformed row.** Guards the campaign UI's ship-count-list formatter against rows with a null `ShipType` key (which vanilla's own formatter can't handle) so one bad entry can't take down an otherwise-valid ship-count display. *(Always on.)*
- **Auto Design no longer wipes a partial layout on a failed attempt.** The constructor's Auto Design (random ship) button previously could burn through several silent retry attempts and leave a partially generated, broken layout behind when they all failed. Fixed to a single clean attempt so a failure can't corrupt the in-progress design. *(Always on.)*
- **Area sea-power reads no longer inflated by fleets just passing through.** Vanilla's area power-projection, escort-power, and raid-power queries counted every vessel the game considered "in" a sea zone, including task forces merely transiting it under movement orders. VP excludes those in-transit vessels from all three calculations so only vessels actually stationed, patrolling, or otherwise present in a zone count toward its power tally. *(Always on, no toggle.)*

## Removed

- **"Obsolete Tech & Hulls (Retain)" feature and its refit-rescue workaround.** Deleted the old opt-in Retain feature (design with already-researched but "obsolete" tech/hulls) and its `ObsoleteRefitRescuePatch` save-recommit workaround, and removed the associated menu toggle. Superseded by the narrower, main-player-only refit-persistence fix; the rescue workaround's re-add-to-designs approach risked GetStore save corruption. (Originally shipped early in the project as "player obsolete design retention"; later replaced entirely.)
- **`CalcFollowing` true-abreast formation override.** Removed — in IL2CPP the native follow-steer calls `CalcFollowing` internally and bypasses the managed Harmony patch, so ships never moved to the clean-abreast station. Abreast is now delivered instead as an offset preset of the Parallel station-keeping order.
- **AI Arms Race (built, never shipped active).** A full design-competitiveness system — per-ship-type effective-power benchmarks and a weighted design-priority nudge meant to stop AI majors clinging to worthless old designs — was built out early in the project but disabled before release; the mod's settings layer now hard-codes it permanently to Off and logs that it "is retired for now." Its supporting code remains in the project but is unreachable in normal play.
